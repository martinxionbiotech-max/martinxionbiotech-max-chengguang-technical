# AGM (Absorbent Glass Mat) Battery

## 1. Definition

An AGM (Absorbent Glass Mat) battery is a valve-regulated lead-acid (VRLA) battery where the electrolyte is absorbed in a fiberglass mat separator, immobilizing the acid and enabling sealed, spill-proof operation with high cycle life.

## 2. How It Works

In an AGM battery, the sulfuric acid electrolyte is fully absorbed into a highly porous borosilicate glass mat compressed between the plates. This 'starved electrolyte' design allows oxygen recombination: oxygen generated at the positive plate during charging travels through the glass mat to the negative plate, where it recombines to form water. This eliminates water loss and enables sealed operation.

## 3. Construction

AGM batteries use compressed cell stacks with tighter plate spacing than flooded batteries. The glass mat separator is compressed to ~20% of its free volume. A pressure relief valve maintains internal pressure at 1-4 psi. Plates use high-purity lead with calcium-tin alloy grids.

## 4. Materials

- Positive plates: Lead dioxide on Pb-Ca-Sn grid
- Negative plates: Sponge lead on Pb-Ca-Sn grid
- Separator: Borosilicate glass microfiber mat (AGM)
- Electrolyte: Absorbed in glass mat (no free liquid)
- Case: High-impact PP with pressure relief valve
- Recombinator catalyst (some designs)

## 5. Technical Parameters

- Voltage: 12V
- CCA: 500–1,000+ A
- Capacity: 50–110 Ah (C20)
- Cycle life: 400–800 cycles (to 50% DoD)
- Self-discharge: 1–3% per month
- Operating temperature: -40degC to +75degC
- Internal resistance: 2–4 mO (lower than flooded)

## 6. Advantages

- 2-3x cycle life vs standard SLI
- Spill-proof and leak-proof (any orientation)
- Low internal resistance for fast charging
- Excellent cold-cranking performance
- Vibration resistant
- Suitable for start-stop vehicles
- No acid stratification
- Lower self-discharge than flooded

## 7. Limitations

- Higher cost (1.5-2x vs SLI)
- Sensitive to overcharging (thermal runaway risk)
- Capacity degrades if deeply discharged and left
- Heavier than EFB
- Requires voltage-controlled charging (14.4-14.8V)

## 8. Applications

- Start-stop vehicles
- Premium/luxury cars
- Vehicles with high electrical loads
- Cold climate regions
- Marine and RV (dual-purpose)
- UPS and backup power
- Motorcycles (powersports)

## 10. Testing

AGM batteries are tested per IEC 60095-6, EN 50342-1 Annex M, and SAE J537. Additional tests include: Dynamic Charge Acceptance (DCA), water consumption (weight loss), high-temperature endurance (EN 50342-1, 60degC), and vibration resistance (IEC 60068-2-6).

## 15. Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I use an AGM battery to replace a standard SLI battery?**

Yes, AGM batteries are direct replacements for flooded batteries. However, ensure the charging system voltage is compatible (14.4-14.8V). Some older vehicles may require charging system adjustments.

**Q: Why are AGM batteries used in start-stop vehicles?**

Start-stop vehicles may restart the engine 20-50 times per trip. AGM batteries handle this cycling load 3-4x better than flooded batteries, and their low internal resistance enables fast recharge between stops.
